Today is National First Responders Day and the team at Early Cardiovascular Health Outreach (ECHO) would like to express our heartfelt gratitude for all the many brave people in law, fire, rescue, medical, and communication emergency services.

We would like to give a special shoutout to our friends at the Honolulu Fire Department. Firefighters don't only help people in fires, they also help people with medical emergencies. In fact, many times, firefighters get called to help with heart-related emergencies like uncontrolled high blood pressure. Because of this, our local fire department has partnered with ECHO to help bring education and experience in an entertaining way to our keiki. We are working together to plan more community events that offer free blood pressure checks and interactive story-telling through [Echo Heroes](/program/echo-heroes). Keep your eyes peeled by checking our [events page](/events) for updates!

Recently, the firefighters also joined us as special guests for [Echo Explorers](/program/echo-explorers). They got into character and took turns acting out parts for [Echo](/characters/Echo), [Artie](/characters/Artie), [Vienna](/characters/Vienna), and [Plaqtrick](/characters/Plaqtrick). The students were enthralled; we all had a blast! The firefighters taught the students how to get their blood pressure checked, and what the numbers mean. This hands-on activity ended with each student being presented their very own ID card called a [Blood Pressure Awareness Card](/activities/BPAC). This card allows space for the students to record their blood pressure reading, and customize their own picture. Students were encouraged to keep their ID and show it to their doctor at their next appointment.

The firefighters are joining us again soon for our current expedition of Echo Explorers taking place this school year!

CEO and Founder of ECHO Kimberly Uehisa has held a special place in her heart for firefighters since she was a teenager. When she was 17 years old and discovered she had high blood pressure, she was advised to monitor it, but she didn’t have a blood pressure monitor. A local fire station offered free checks, and she took advantage of the opportunity. Fire stations carry the equipment necessary for youth blood pressure screenings, which includes smaller cuffs for the machines to ensure accurate results. Kim was grateful for the help, because after some time and more readings, she discovered that her high blood pressure was actually hypertension—chronic high blood pressure. With early intervention, she was able to get the medical help she required to live a healthy life. During this journey, she learned heart-healthy habits that she continues to use today to help her maintain a balanced lifestyle. Kim experienced first-hand the importance of youth education on heart health, and has now built ECHO to spread awareness across the island. By combining the efforts of the fire department and ECHO, early blood pressure screenings will not only be known, but will also be celebrated!
